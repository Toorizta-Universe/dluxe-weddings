# PRODUCT.md — D'Luxe Weddings & Events

## Register
brand

## Product Purpose
A multi-page lead-capture website for D'Luxe Weddings & Events, a luxury wedding planning and event design studio based in Costa Rica. The site introduces founder Madelyn Sánchez, communicates the studio's bespoke (no-package) philosophy, walks couples through the planning process, and converts visitors into qualified inquiries via a vision questionnaire.

## Users
- Engaged couples planning a luxury or destination wedding, typically with $50k–$200k+ budgets
- Often North American (English-speaking), considering Costa Rica or Latin America destinations
- Sometimes Latin American couples planning at home; bilingual ceremony is a differentiator
- Decision-makers who care more about taste, story, and trust than pricing — they self-select on aesthetic
- They are visual buyers. The site IS the pitch.

## Brand Voice
- Warm, considered, intimate. Spoken not stated.
- Italics carry feeling. Plain text carries trust.
- Confident without performance — "designed exclusively for them" not "the best in the industry."
- Specific over abstract: "the moment after the first dance" beats "creating memories."
- Bilingual sensibility — never translates clumsily, never name-drops Spanish for flavor.

## Tone
- Editorial, not promotional.
- Quietly luxe. Money implied, never invoked.
- Romantic but unsentimental. No "fairy tale," no "magical day."

## Strategic Principles
- The site is the first sales conversation. Every section earns its presence.
- Photography leads, copy follows. Words live around images, not over them.
- Personalization is the product. Every section reinforces that nothing here is templated.
- Costa Rica is context, not theme. The work is luxury wedding design that happens to be set in the tropics, not "tropical wedding planner."
- The questionnaire is the conversion event. Everything routes there with intent.

## Anti-references
- **Generic wedding-industry web aesthetic:** cream backgrounds with thin gold serif italics and watercolor florals. This is the dominant cliché and we explicitly avoid it as the protagonist of the design.
- **Stock-feeling icon + heading + paragraph card grids.** No service grids. No "our process" 3-up grids.
- **SaaS hero pattern:** eyebrow, h1, subhead, two-button row, hero image right. Recognizable from a mile away.
- **Resort-website florals:** corner-decoration palm-leaf SVGs as ambient garnish.
- **Gradient text, glassmorphism, "luxury" gold gradients.** Never.

## What "good" looks like
A reader scrolls and feels: this person has taste, this person has done this many times, this person will protect my day. The site looks like an art-directed editorial feature on Madelyn's studio, not a service brochure. When they reach the questionnaire, they are already half-committed.
