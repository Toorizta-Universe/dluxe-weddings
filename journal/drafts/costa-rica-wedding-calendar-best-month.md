# The Costa Rica wedding calendar: the *best* month to marry, by region

*By Madelyn Sánchez · INIBEP-certified Wedding Planner · May 12, 2026 · 15 min read · Planning Guides*

> **The short answer.** Costa Rica has two seasons. Dry season (December through April) is the safest weather bet for any region and the peak of wedding demand — higher pricing, longer lead times, lowest weather risk. Green season (May through November) trades 10–20% lower venue pricing for weather risk that varies dramatically by region. **Guanacaste is the driest part of the country, even inside green season. Manuel Antonio and the South Pacific are the wettest.** The right month for your wedding depends almost entirely on which coast you're standing on. Below: a month-by-month, region-by-region calendar, three "best month for…" recommendations, an honest weather-budget conversation, and an FAQ.

---

I have produced weddings in every calendar month, in every region of Costa Rica. February dawns in Tamarindo where the trade winds will not stop until you stake the linens. June afternoons at a private villa in Las Catalinas where it rained for forty-five minutes at 3pm, exactly when I'd told the couple it would, and then the sky cleared and the sunset was the most beautiful one I have ever produced a wedding under. The first real rain of the year at Manuel Antonio in late April, which arrives one evening with no announcement and rearranges everything for the next six months. November in Papagayo, the deepest "green" by paper definition, with a week of skies so clean I had three couples in a row tell me they didn't believe me when I'd warned them about clouds.

This is the version of the seasonal conversation I would have with you in person. It is the version most wedding websites won't give you because the easy answer is "December to April" and the easy answer sells more shoulder-season inquiries. I would rather you marry in the right month for the *right* region than book a dry-season Saturday and never know that your same wedding, in late October, would have cost 15% less and been just as beautiful.

A note before we begin: every weather pattern described here is based on the weddings I have actually produced and the regional micro-climates I have stood inside of. Costa Rica is a small country with extraordinarily varied weather — your honeymoon in Guanacaste and a friend's honeymoon in Manuel Antonio the same week can be entirely different vacations. Plan accordingly.

## <a id="two-seasons"></a>The two seasons, honestly explained

Costa Rica has two seasons, not four. *Verano* (summer) and *invierno* (winter) — and they sit roughly opposite to the U.S. and European calendars, which trips up half the couples I work with.

**Dry season — verano — December through April.** This is the country's "summer." The skies are clean, the trade winds are reliable, the rivers are low. It is also the country's high tourism season — flights are more expensive, hotels are more expensive, and the best wedding venues book 14 to 18 months ahead. Weather risk is low. Demand risk is high.

**Green season — invierno — May through November.** This is the country's "winter." The land turns vivid green. Mornings are clear and bright. Afternoons bring rain — sometimes a fifteen-minute downpour, sometimes a longer storm — and evenings clear back out, often beautifully. Hotels discount. Vendors are more available. Some weeks are so dry you would not know it was the rainy season. Other weeks, especially September and October at certain venues, can be genuinely wet.

That is the country-wide pattern. It is also where most blog posts stop, and where most couples get into trouble. The country-wide pattern is not the wedding planner's truth. The wedding planner's truth is the regional one.

## <a id="regional"></a>Regional weather is not uniform — the whole thesis of this piece

If you remember nothing else from this post, remember this: **Costa Rica's weather is not the same on both coasts, or in both halves of the same coast.** A "rainy" month in Manuel Antonio can be a perfect month in Tamarindo, four hours away. The country is small enough that this feels surprising, and large enough — in terrain — that it is genuinely true.

Here is the regional shorthand I work from, region by region.

### Guanacaste — Tamarindo, Papagayo, Las Catalinas, Playa Conchal, Playa Hermosa

The driest region of the country. Guanacaste sits in a rain shadow created by the country's central mountain range, and it gets significantly less rainfall year-round than the rest of the Pacific coast. *Even in green season*, Guanacaste's rain pattern is mostly: clear morning, mid-afternoon shower, clear evening. May, June, and November can be entirely usable for outdoor weddings. The "deep green" window of September–October is the wettest, but even then the rain is rarely all-day. Trade winds are strongest December through March — strong enough that beach ceremonies need wind-aware design, but the air is dry and the skies are reliably clean.

This is where I would recommend any couple worried about weather marry — especially in late October, early November, or late April. The "shoulder season" in Guanacaste is the planner's secret.

### Central Pacific — Manuel Antonio, Jacó, Quepos

A significantly wetter region than Guanacaste. The terrain shifts here — the mountains come closer to the coast, the rainforest is denser, and the rain pattern is more aggressive. May through July is moderate green season — mostly afternoon rain, mornings and evenings clear. **August through October is genuinely wet.** I have produced weddings at Manuel Antonio in October that were entirely outdoor and beautiful; I have also produced one where it rained for eleven hours straight, and the tent contingency was the only thing that kept the night from collapsing. December through April is clean and lovely.

If you are set on Manuel Antonio, my honest recommendation is January through early April, or late November through early December. Anything else, plan the rain into the design.

### South Pacific — Uvita, Dominical, the Osa Peninsula

The wettest of the regions I produce weddings in. The Osa Peninsula is one of the rainiest parts of Costa Rica, full stop. **September and October are the wettest months of the year here.** December through March is the clean window. April begins to soften. May onward is real rain, often heavy, often beautiful, almost never compatible with the open-air design most couples want.

I will work with couples on the South Pacific for jungle weddings — the immersive Osa rainforest experience is genuinely singular — but I will only recommend December through March for an outdoor ceremony without backup. Outside that window, plan an indoor-or-tent thesis from day one.

### Caribbean coast — Puerto Viejo, Cahuita

The Caribbean coast runs on a different calendar entirely. **Its driest months are September and October** — the exact months when the Pacific is at its wettest. Its wettest months are December and January. I include this for completeness, because most couples don't know the Caribbean inverts the country's pattern. In practice, the Caribbean is rarely a luxury wedding destination — the road, the lodging infrastructure, and the vendor depth are not there yet — but if you have a reason to consider it, plan around its own calendar, not the Pacific's.

### Central Valley — San José, Heredia, Atenas, the coffee estates

The Central Valley sits at altitude (around 3,000–4,500 feet) and stays mild year-round. Daytime highs hover in the 70s and 80s, evenings drop into the 60s, and rain follows the green-season pattern but is gentler. This is mountain weather, and the wedding work that happens here is mostly hacienda and coffee-estate work — not a primary luxury beach-wedding zone, but a beautiful option for couples who want cooler temperatures and an old-world Costa Rica feel. April through November can produce afternoon rain; December through March is most reliable.

## <a id="months"></a>The twelve months, written out

What the table can't carry is the *feeling* of each month — how the light moves, what's in bloom, which holidays will close the country, what the demand and pricing tell you about your lead time. Here, month by month.

### January — peak season, deepest dry

January in Guanacaste is the cleanest weather window of the year. Trade winds are strong but the air is dry, the sky is clean, the sunsets are reliable. Mornings break with a sharp clarity you don't get later in the year. Central Pacific and South Pacific are also dry — this is one of the few months I would book an outdoor ceremony anywhere in the country without a tent.

The cost: this is peak season. Venues are at their highest pricing. Top private estates — The Point, Vista Hermosa Estate, Casa Chameleon, Villa Punto de Vista, Castle of Oz — book 14 to 18 months out for January Saturdays. Vendor availability tightens. Plan accordingly.

What's in bloom: tabebuia (the yellow corteza amarilla) is beginning its dry-season flowering through the inland valleys. Local heliconia and tropical foliage are abundant year-round.

### February — driest, windiest

February is the driest month of the year. It is also, for Guanacaste, the windiest — trade winds peak. Beach ceremonies require wind-aware design (no loose linens, no unsecured candles, hair pinned harder than the bride is used to). Photographers know how to use this wind beautifully; I have shot some of my favorite veil shots in February.

Central Pacific and South Pacific lose some of the trade-wind intensity Guanacaste has, and they are equally dry. February in Manuel Antonio is one of the country's most reliable wedding months.

Demand is at peak. Valentine's weekend in particular pushes pricing on photographers and select florists. If you have flexibility, marry the weekend after.

### March — hottest, last reliably dry month

March is hot. Inland temperatures can reach the high 90s; the coast moderates with sea breeze but the midday sun is genuinely strong. Plan ceremony times accordingly — a 4pm ceremony in Guanacaste in March can be uncomfortable for guests on a beach without shade. I move most March ceremonies to 4:30 or 5pm.

This is the last fully reliable dry month in much of the country. Late March still feels like winter (which here means clear and dry) but the energy is already beginning to shift toward the transition.

Spring break demand from the U.S. layers onto wedding demand. Lodging is at its tightest. Book early or marry mid-week.

### April — the transition, watch Semana Santa

Early April is still dry. The light is golden, the country is parched-green, the rivers run low. **Late April is when the first real rain typically arrives** — sometimes a dramatic evening downpour that announces itself, sometimes a series of soft afternoon showers that build over a week. The exact date varies year to year; in 2024 the first real rain hit Manuel Antonio on April 25, in 2023 it was May 9.

The big honest flag for April: **Semana Santa (Easter Week / Holy Week)**. Most of Costa Rica essentially closes for the week leading into and including Easter Sunday. Many vendors are unavailable. Lodging is booked solid with domestic tourism. Pricing surges. I strongly recommend marrying *outside* Semana Santa for any April wedding — check the dates each year and plan around them.

### May — green season begins, Guanacaste still workable

May is where the regional differences begin to matter intensely. In **Guanacaste**, May is still mostly dry — the first rains arrive in the afternoon, often a 30-minute shower at 3 or 4pm, and clear by ceremony time. May ceremonies in Tamarindo or Papagayo are some of the most beautiful I produce: the land has just been refreshed by the first rains, everything turns vivid green within ten days, and the light is softer than the harder dry-season clarity.

In **Central Pacific** (Manuel Antonio, Jacó), May is moderate green — clear mornings, frequent afternoon rain, evenings usually clearing. Outdoor ceremonies are still workable with attentive design and a tent contingency.

In **South Pacific** (Uvita, Osa), May is real rain. I would not recommend it for outdoor weddings without a covered backup as the primary plan.

Pricing: 10–20% venue relief begins in May. Vendor availability is meaningfully better. This is the start of the "wedding planner's value window."

### June — the planner's hidden Guanacaste gem

June is the month I quietly recommend to couples who want a Guanacaste wedding at meaningful savings. The pattern in Tamarindo, Papagayo, and Las Catalinas in June is reliable: clear sunrise, sunny morning, building cumulus by noon, afternoon shower between 2 and 5pm, clearing by sunset. Ceremonies at 4:30–5:30pm consistently catch the clear-evening window. Sunsets in late June in Guanacaste are some of the most cinematic of the year — the post-shower atmosphere makes the light do things you cannot manufacture.

Manuel Antonio in June is wetter than Guanacaste; South Pacific is wetter still. The regional rule holds harder in June than in May.

What it costs: this is the green-season-bargain sweet spot. Venues, photographers, and most vendors are at green-season pricing. Lead times shorten dramatically — I have built 6-month June weddings that did not feel rushed.

### July — green with a mid-month surprise

Costa Rica has a phenomenon called the *veranillo de San Juan* — a small dry window that often arrives in mid-July, somewhere between July 10 and July 25, lasting four to ten days. Skies clear, the air dries, and the country gets a brief reset before the deeper rains of August and September. The exact timing varies year to year and is not something to plan a wedding around — but if you are already producing a wedding in July and the veranillo lines up, it is a gift.

Outside the veranillo, July is moderate-to-heavy green season everywhere outside Guanacaste. Guanacaste in July remains the most usable region.

### August — Guanacaste workable, elsewhere genuinely wet

August begins the deeper green season. Guanacaste is still workable — the pattern is wetter than June but the trade-wind structure keeps mornings and evenings usable. Central Pacific and South Pacific are entering their wettest stretch.

Demand softens further. Many of the top venues will quote noticeably better pricing in August than in March. If you are flexible on region and you want a private-estate wedding for a smaller budget, this is where I would look.

### September — the wettest Pacific month, the cheapest pricing

September is the wettest month on the Pacific coast. In Guanacaste, the pattern shifts from afternoon-shower to longer storms, sometimes morning, sometimes all-day. Ceremonies are still producible with tent contingency, but the open-air-thesis is harder to defend. In Manuel Antonio and Uvita, September is the height of green — and the height of the rain.

This is the cheapest month to marry in Costa Rica. The deepest discounts I have ever negotiated for couples have been September weddings. If you are pricing-sensitive, value-seeking, willing to design for weather rather than against it, and you understand the tradeoff, September is the bargain.

The country flip-side: the Caribbean coast is at its driest in September. If you have a reason to go Caribbean, this is the window.

### October — Pacific still wet, Guanacaste shoulder begins

Early October mirrors September on the Pacific — wet. **But late October is the shoulder.** Guanacaste's rain pattern starts to ease in the final week. The "deep green" Pacific outside Guanacaste is still very wet through mid-October — I would not book an outdoor Manuel Antonio or Uvita wedding in October without a tent as the primary plan.

The last week of October in Guanacaste is one of my favorite quiet recommendations. The land is at its most lush after the green season, demand has not yet returned, pricing is still in green-season territory, and the weather is more dry than wet. Couples who book this window often get the best of both seasons.

### November — Guanacaste re-opens, the bargain windows close

November is the most important transitional month for couples who want a value wedding. In **Guanacaste**, November is mostly dry — particularly the first three weeks. The trade winds return, the skies clean up, and pricing remains in green-season territory through about mid-month before December rates and demand begin to surge.

**Central Pacific** dries through the month — early November can still see rain, late November is clean. **South Pacific** is still wet in early November, transitioning by late month.

November in Guanacaste is the answer to the question "what's the best green-season bargain for a couple who doesn't want to gamble on weather?" It is the month I most often recommend to couples in that conversation.

A holiday flag: U.S. Thanksgiving falls in late November. Some American couples like to marry the week after Thanksgiving so guests can extend the holiday into the wedding. The trade-off is slightly higher lodging demand from holiday travelers.

### December — dry season returns, holiday demand spikes

December is dry across most of the country. Guanacaste, Central Pacific, and South Pacific all return to clean weather. The grass is still bright green from the November rain — December has some of the most photogenic landscape of the year.

The cost: December is peak holiday demand. The week between Christmas and New Year is the highest-priced wedding week of the year. Lodging surges. Flight pricing surges. If you want December, marry the first two weeks (when the weather is dry but the holiday traffic hasn't arrived) and book 16+ months out.

What's in bloom: the country's flame trees (poró, malinche) flower brilliantly in December. Local florists can build extraordinary tropical work this month.

## <a id="best-tamarindo"></a>Best month for a Tamarindo / Guanacaste wedding

**February or March.** Peak dry, peak everything. If you want the cleanest possible weather, the most reliable sunset light, and the certainty that nothing about the calendar will surprise you, you marry in late February or early March in Guanacaste. You pay peak pricing for it, and you book 14 to 18 months out. The trade is straightforward and worth it for couples who do not want to think about weather at all.

The one caveat: trade winds. February is the windiest month of the year on the Pacific coast. Build for it — secured linens, weighted floral, hair plans that account for the breeze. The wind is part of the Pacific Costa Rica feeling and the best photographers know how to work with it.

## <a id="best-bargain"></a>Best month for a green-season bargain (the planner's secret)

**Late October to early November, in Guanacaste.** This is the answer most couples never get told. The dry season has not yet returned, but the deep green has begun to end. Guanacaste is mostly clear, the land is at its most lush, pricing is still in green-season territory (10–20% under February), and demand is at its lowest before the December surge.

Same recommendation does *not* apply to Manuel Antonio or Uvita in late October — those regions are still genuinely wet. The bargain window is regionally specific to Guanacaste, and it closes by mid-November as dry-season pricing returns.

If a couple comes to me wanting the look of a peak-season wedding for noticeably less money, this is where I send them.

## <a id="best-bargain-rain"></a>Best month for couples who want fewer guests, lower cost, and don't mind some rain

**June, in Guanacaste.** June is the most consistently *workable* deep green-season month in the country's driest region. The rain pattern is predictable — clear mornings, afternoon shower, clear evenings — and the savings are real. I have built 6-month June weddings on tight timelines that felt as designed as 18-month February weddings.

The honest tradeoff: you build the design with the rain in mind. Tent contingency is real. The ceremony time is set to catch the post-shower clearing. The dance floor has a plan for moisture. Couples who lean into the green season — who want the lushness, who don't mind being told to keep an umbrella nearby, who like the cinematic quality of a post-rain golden hour — get something quietly extraordinary in June.

## <a id="tent-budget"></a>What to budget for weather — the tent contingency

This is the section every couple wants me to skip, and the one I will never let them.

If you are marrying in green season (May through November) anywhere in Costa Rica, or in late April or early December in transitional weather, **you build a tent contingency into the budget from day one**. Not as an option. As a line item.

The honest range for a real tent contingency at a luxury wedding in Costa Rica:

- **$4,000–$6,000** for a smaller wedding (40–60 guests), modest tent, basic flooring, lighting integration
- **$6,000–$9,000** for a mid-size wedding (70–90 guests), pole tent or clearspan, more refined flooring, integrated lighting
- **$9,000–$12,000+** for a full-estate wedding (100+ guests), clearspan structure, climate considerations, full-design integration that does not feel like a "backup"

A real tent at the luxury tier is not a plastic emergency cover. It is a structure designed to enhance the wedding if used, designed to feel intentional, often with sides that open if the weather clears. The best green-season weddings I have produced were ones where the tent was the design from the beginning — and where it did not rain, and the tent was still beautiful.

The single biggest mistake I see couples make: skipping the tent contingency to save $5,000, then paying $400 cash for an emergency canopy rental at 4pm on the day of the wedding because the sky opened. The 13% IVA on a real tent is built in. The 4pm emergency rental is not.

If you marry in dry season (December through April), the tent contingency is genuinely optional. Even there, I usually budget $2,000–$3,000 for a small overhead-cover contingency at outdoor venues — Costa Rica's microclimates surprise people.

## A note on Semana Santa — the week to avoid

Every April, Costa Rica observes **Semana Santa** (Holy Week / Easter Week), the week leading into Easter Sunday. Most of the country closes. Banks, government offices, many restaurants, much of the vendor community — closed. Local lodging is booked solid with Tico domestic tourism. Pricing surges across hotels and flights. Many of the best vendors I work with take the entire week off and will not produce a wedding during Semana Santa for any price.

I recommend avoiding Semana Santa entirely. Check the dates each year (Easter 2026 is April 5; Easter 2027 is March 28; Easter 2028 is April 16) and plan around them.

## <a id="faq"></a>Frequently asked questions

**When is the best time to get married in Costa Rica?**
Dry season — December through April — is the safest weather window across the entire country. Within that, February and March are the cleanest, driest, and most reliable. They are also the most expensive and the most booked. For couples seeking better value with low weather risk, late October to early November in Guanacaste is the planner's quiet favorite.

**Is May a good month for a Costa Rica wedding?**
In Guanacaste, yes — May is mostly dry, with afternoon showers that rarely interfere with evening ceremonies. May ceremonies in Tamarindo, Papagayo, and Las Catalinas are some of the most beautiful I produce. In Manuel Antonio and the South Pacific, May begins to be genuinely wet — plan accordingly.

**When is the rainy season in Costa Rica?**
The rainy (green) season runs from May through November on the Pacific coast and through the Central Valley. The Caribbean coast runs on a different calendar — its driest months are September and October. The wettest months on the Pacific are September and October.

**Is October too rainy for a Costa Rica wedding?**
For Manuel Antonio, Uvita, and the South Pacific — yes, October is genuinely wet. For Guanacaste, October is wetter than February but still produces beautiful weddings, especially in the final week of the month when the shoulder season begins.

**What is Costa Rica's peak wedding season?**
December through April. Within that, January through March is the peak of the peak. Demand and pricing are highest, top venues book 14 to 18 months ahead, and weather risk is lowest.

**Should I avoid Easter Week (Semana Santa) for a wedding?**
Yes. Most of Costa Rica closes for Semana Santa. Many vendors are unavailable. Lodging is booked solid with domestic tourism. Pricing surges. Check the date each year and plan around it.

**Is December a good month for a Costa Rica wedding?**
December is excellent for weather — dry across the country, with the landscape still lush from the November rain. The trade-off is holiday demand: the week between Christmas and New Year is the most expensive wedding week of the year. If you want December, marry the first two weeks and book early.

**When does it stop raining in Costa Rica?**
In Guanacaste, the rain meaningfully eases by mid-to-late November. In Central Pacific, by mid-to-late November. In South Pacific, by early December. The country is fully into dry season by December 10 in most years.

**Is February or March the best month for a Costa Rica wedding?**
Both are excellent and broadly equivalent for weather. February is windier (peak trade winds); March is hotter (inland highs in the 90s). February gives you slightly more reliable sunsets; March gives you more guest-comfortable temperatures along the coast. I marry slightly more couples in March than February for that reason.

**Should I have my wedding in green season to save money?**
If you understand the trade-off and you build a real tent contingency, yes — green season can save 10–20% on venue and offer better vendor availability. The wrong way to use green season is to assume "it might not rain." The right way is to design with the rain in mind, choose a region (Guanacaste) where the rain is more predictable, and budget the tent.

**Is November dry in Costa Rica?**
The first three weeks of November in Guanacaste are mostly dry. Central Pacific dries through the month. South Pacific lags about two weeks behind. November is the most important transitional month for couples seeking shoulder-season value in Guanacaste.

**What's the windiest month in Costa Rica?**
February in Guanacaste, with January and early March close behind. Trade winds are strong, the air is dry, and beach ceremonies require wind-aware design (no loose linens, weighted floral, hair plans built for the breeze).

**Is the Caribbean coast a good wedding destination?**
For most luxury couples, no — the road, the lodging infrastructure, and the vendor depth are not at the level needed to produce a luxury wedding. The Caribbean's biggest selling point is that it runs on an inverted calendar (driest in September and October), but the broader logistics make it a niche choice. Most of our work is on the Pacific coast.

**How does weather differ between Tamarindo and Manuel Antonio?**
Significantly. Tamarindo (Guanacaste) is the country's driest wedding region, with reliable trade winds and predictable rain patterns even in green season. Manuel Antonio (Central Pacific) is noticeably wetter — its peak rain months (August through October) are genuinely wet, while Tamarindo in the same months is mostly afternoon-shower. The two regions are four hours apart by road and feel like different climates.

**What month has the best light for a Costa Rica wedding?**
Late June through July in Guanacaste, post-shower. The atmospheric quality after a green-season rain produces some of the most cinematic golden-hour light I have ever produced a wedding under. February's light is cleaner but harder; June's light is softer and more layered.

**Do I need a backup plan for an outdoor ceremony in February?**
Not seriously — February in Guanacaste is the safest weather month of the year, and most of my February ceremonies are produced fully outdoors without a tent. I do still budget a $2,000–$3,000 small-cover contingency at outdoor venues for peace of mind; Costa Rica's microclimates have surprised me enough times that I do not produce a wedding without one.

**Can I get married in the rainy season and still have an outdoor ceremony?**
Yes — but you build the design around the rain pattern, not against it. In Guanacaste, schedule the ceremony for 4:30–5:30pm to catch the post-shower clearing. Design with a tent contingency that enhances rather than emergency-covers. Choose a venue with covered alternative spaces (a covered terrace, a great room) that feels designed if you need to move. The best green-season weddings are produced with both plans in mind from day one.

**What is the best month for a green-season wedding in Costa Rica?**
June in Guanacaste, by my preference. Predictable rain pattern, deep savings on pricing, generous vendor availability, and post-shower light that produces some of my favorite weddings of the year. Late October to early November is the alternative for couples who want even less rain risk while staying in green-season pricing.

## What I would tell you if we were sitting across from each other right now

The honest truth about Costa Rica's wedding calendar is that *region matters more than month*. A February wedding in Uvita and a February wedding in Tamarindo are the same date and entirely different events. A June wedding in Tamarindo and a June wedding in Manuel Antonio are entirely different bets. The country is small enough that this feels surprising, and varied enough — in terrain, in mountains, in coast — that it is genuinely true.

If you have a region you are set on, marry in its best month. If you have a month you are set on, marry in the region that month is best for. If you have neither — you just want a beautiful wedding in Costa Rica and you want to make the right call — let me know your budget and your weather tolerance, and I can place you on a date in a region inside of thirty minutes.

The calendar is not the wedding. The wedding is the design, the vendors, the way the day flows, the people you love eating dinner together with the sound of the Pacific in the background. The calendar is the frame around it. Pick the right frame and the work inside it gets to be what it should be.
